3. 🌪️ Clima & Catástrofes

Verisk estimates Isaias insured losses at $1-2bn, driven largely by wind damage

Verisk’s Catastrophe and Risk Solutions business has estimated insured industry losses to onshore property from Hurricane Isaias at between $1 billion and $2 billion, with wind damage expected to account for the vast majority of losses. Verisk explained that the estimate covers wind and privately insured storm surge losses from Isaias’ Florida Panhandle landfall. According to the firm, the most severe wind damage occurred west of Isaias’ landfall location, particularly in Florida’s Santa Rosa and northeastern Escambia counties.

Hurricane Simon track remained outside of Mexico cat bond parametric trigger zones: Loconte

As major hurricane Simon’s path shifted west it appears to have remained offshore of the important parametric trigger zones related to the $175 million IBRD CAR Mexico 2024 (Pacific) catastrophe bond, according to analysis by Isacco Loconte an insurance-linked securities (ILS) Investment Specialist at asset manager Azimut Switzerland SA. As we had been reporting over the weekend , hurricane Simon’s minimum central pressure, a critical factor in determining whether any payout would come due for the catastrophe bond, did deepen to a sufficiently low level to activate a payout should the storm’s track have crossed into any of the parametric trigger boxes. While those pressure levels were strong enough to meet the L1 payout threshold for the parametric zones, the hurricane trajectory worked in the bond’s favor.” Based on the parametric trigger design, hurricane Simon’s central pressure would have needed to be at 932mb or lower to activate a payout from the zones it came closest to.

5. 🤝 M&A

Global M&A activity increases in 9M’26, reports WTW

Global mergers and acquisitions (M&A) activity increased in the first nine months of 2026, with higher-value transactions contributing to a rise in overall deal value, according to WTW, the global professional services company providing risk, insurance, investment and consulting services. WTW reported that activity accelerated during the third quarter, with nine mega deals completed compared with three in the previous quarter. Activity among deals valued between USD $1 billion and USD $10 billion was more subdued.
