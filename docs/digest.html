Inszone acquires Washington employee benefits agency Radwick & Lightle

Inszone Insurance has acquired The Lightle Group, LLC, doing business as Radwick & Lightle, expanding its employee benefits operations in Washington. The acquisition brings Radwick & Lightle’s employee benefits team into Inszone, providing access to a broader range of insurance markets, technology, and operational resources while maintaining the agency’s existing client service model. Founded in 2001 by Curt Lightle and based in Bremerton, Washington, Radwick & Lightle specializes in employee benefits for small and midsize businesses, offering benefits consulting, HR support, compliance services, and claims advocacy.

3. 🌪️ Clima & Catástrofes

Global data centre insurance market projected to exceed $24bn by 2030: Allianz Commercial

The global data centre insurance market is projected to grow from currently around US $11 billion to more than $24 billion by 2030, which comes as the market continues to drive an influx of infrastructure risks and opportunities, according to Allianz Commercial, the commercial insurance business of Allianz Group. There have been comments made by senior industry leaders that capacity may be insufficient to support all the data centre builds expected to come online over the next few years, with the insurance-linked securities market seen as a viable source of incremental reinsurance capacity to support this. Thomas Lillelund, CEO of Allianz Commercial, commented: “AI is turning the latest generation of data centers from a specialist real estate asset into mission-critical infrastructure.” “The scale of investment is extraordinary and, as these centers evolve beyond traditional data storage to high-performance compute demands, success will increasingly depend on resilience: access to power, reliable supply chains, robust construction controls, as well as climate-aware site selection and insurance programs that reflect the true accumulation risk.

4. 💰 Inversión

Cytix raises $7M to manage security risks from AI-driven software development

“Existing tools can tell you what vulnerabilities you have, but can’t tell you about the risk.” Cytix’s platform continuously monitors software changes, assesses the security risks they create and determines the appropriate response. The platform sits between software development teams and an organisation’s security, risk and compliance functions, providing a central view of what has changed, the potential business impact and how the resulting risks were handled. Cybersecurity startup Cytix has raised $7 million in Series A funding to scale its change risk management platform as AI-assisted software development accelerates the pace and volume of code changes.
