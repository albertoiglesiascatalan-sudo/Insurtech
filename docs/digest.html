1. ⚖️ Regulación

Ceres supports California proposal on long-term insurance solvency planning

In a comment letter submitted to the California Department of Insurance, Ceres said the proposed Long-Term Solvency Planning Regulation would strengthen oversight of the state’s insurance sector while bringing it into line with solvency and transition-planning approaches already adopted by regulators in other parts of the world. Ceres, a US-based non-profit organisation that works with investors, companies and policymakers to advance sustainable business practices and improve the management of climate-related financial risks, has voiced its support for a proposed California regulation that would require the state’s largest domestic insurers to prepare long-term solvency plans addressing financial risks, including those linked to extreme weather. Commenting on the proposal, Steven Rothstein, Chief Programme Officer at Ceres, said the regulation would provide “much needed visibility into how carriers are preparing for future risk.” He added that California’s residents, communities and businesses needed “an insurance market capable of weathering the climate and technology challenges ahead,” describing the proposed framework as an important step towards building that resilience.

3. 🤝 M&A

Inszone acquires Washington employee benefits agency Radwick & Lightle

Inszone Insurance has acquired The Lightle Group, LLC, doing business as Radwick & Lightle, expanding its employee benefits operations in Washington. The acquisition brings Radwick & Lightle’s employee benefits team into Inszone, providing access to a broader range of insurance markets, technology, and operational resources while maintaining the agency’s existing client service model. Founded in 2001 by Curt Lightle and based in Bremerton, Washington, Radwick & Lightle specializes in employee benefits for small and midsize businesses, offering benefits consulting, HR support, compliance services, and claims advocacy.

5. 💰 Inversión $150M

HappyRobot raises $150 million

HappyRobot, a company building “enterprise superintelligence,” has raised $150 million in Series C funding led by Prysm Capital and co-led by Eurazeo. The round values the company at $1.2 billion post-money, bringing total funding to around $200 million. After first “proving” its platform in logistics, HappyRobot is expanding across the supply chain as well as into insurance, energy and utilities, telecommunications, airlines, and other sectors where business-critical work still depends on manual coordination across fragmented systems.
