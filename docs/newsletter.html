EU unveils climate resilience framework with re/insurance protection gap in focus

The European Commission has presented an EU Framework for Climate Resilience to strengthen Europe’s ability to anticipate, prepare for and manage climate risks, highlighting re/insurance as a “crucial element” in this equation. The framework reportedly aims to embed climate resilience across EU policies, support local and regional authorities, and encourage private-sector investment in adaptation, with an estimated €70 billion a year needed to adapt Europe’s infrastructure and economy. We are ensuring that climate resilience will be included in policies across the continent, supporting investment, innovation and long-term growth.” Alongside the framework, the Commission has proposed a Climate Resilience Law to establish common assumptions for future global warming, simplify national planning through a single strategic plan for each member state, and improve the consistency of climate risk assessments.

EU Commission proposes climate resilience framework, highlights cat bonds & reinsurance

The European Commission has called for a new climate resilience framework to strengthen Europe’s ability to anticipate, prepare for, and manage climate risks. “The EU Climate Resilience Framework aims to strengthen Europe’s ability to anticipate, prepare for, and manage climate risks by getting ahead of those impacts and avoiding damages through coordinated action at EU, national and local levels,” the Commission explained. The Framework combines a proposed European Climate Resilience Law with practical tools and supporting measures to help public authorities, businesses, and other stakeholders make better-informed decisions.” As mentioned, the proposal explains that the European Commission will set up a Climate Insurance Alliance, to help improve insurance protection and support risk prevention across Europe.

Morocco targets regulatory framework for insurance securitization, including cat bonds

The government of Morocco is now aiming to prepare a regulatory framework to allow for the securitization of insurance risks, including through catastrophe bonds, as the country seemingly aims to support issuance as well as to be a beneficiary of risk transfer to the capital markets. The new Finance Bill for fiscal year 2027 calls for the preparation of a regulatory framework for the securitization of insurance risks, including catastrophe bonds. It’s particularly notable to see the mention of Morocco having a goal to construct its own regulatory framework for the securitization of insurance risks, as a way to bring third-party capital more directly into the country to support natural disaster, catastrophe and climate risk transfer.

4. 💰 Inversión

Mapfre Re’s 9M’26 profit climbs 11.8% to €286m

Mapfre Re, Mapfre’s Reinsurance and Global Risks arm, grew profit 11.8% to €286 million in the first nine months of 2026, helped by a 67.8% rise in its Non-Life financial result, even as its combined ratio worsened and it absorbed the impact of the earthquake in Colombia. At group level, Mapfre’s net profit for the first nine months of 2026 rose 11.5% to €924 million, while total premiums grew 2% to €22.8 billion, with Life up 5% and Non-Life up 1.1%. Mapfre Re’s premiums were broadly steady at €6.3 billion in the first nine months of 2026, with Mapfre citing declining reinsurance and insurance rates.

  1. El dato clave: Mapfre Re’s 9M’26 profit climbs 11.8% to €286m.
