2. 🌪️ Clima & Catástrofes

Verisk estimates Isaias insured losses at $1-2bn, driven largely by wind damage

Verisk’s Catastrophe and Risk Solutions business has estimated insured industry losses to onshore property from Hurricane Isaias at between $1 billion and $2 billion, with wind damage expected to account for the vast majority of losses. Verisk explained that the estimate covers wind and privately insured storm surge losses from Isaias’ Florida Panhandle landfall. According to the firm, the most severe wind damage occurred west of Isaias’ landfall location, particularly in Florida’s Santa Rosa and northeastern Escambia counties.

  1. El contexto: Verisk explained that the estimate covers wind and privately insured storm surge losses from Isaias’ Florida Panhandle landfall.
  2. ¿Por qué importa? Impacto directo en exposición, pricing y reservas del sector.
  3. ¿Qué vigilar? El sector deberá revisar modelos de pricing y cobertura en las zonas afectadas ante el incremento de eventos extremos.

As we had been reporting over the weekend , hurricane Simon’s minimum central pressure, a critical factor in determining whether any payout would come due for the catastrophe bond, did deepen to a sufficiently low level to activate a payout should the storm’s track have crossed into any of the parametric trigger boxes. While those pressure levels were strong enough to meet the L1 payout threshold for the parametric zones, the hurricane trajectory worked in the bond’s favor.” Based on the parametric trigger design, hurricane Simon’s central pressure would have needed to be at 932mb or lower to activate a payout from the zones it came closest to.
