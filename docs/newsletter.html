1. 🌪️ Clima & Catástrofes

Verisk estimates Hurricane Isaias insured onshore property losses in $1bn to $2bn range

Verisk, a catastrophe risk modelling and data analytics specialist, has now issued an initial industry insured loss estimate for recent hurricane Isaias, saying that onshore property losses from the storm could reach $2 billion. Verisk’s initial industry loss estimate includes losses from wind and privately insured storm surge, as a result of hurricane Isaias’ landfall in the Florida Panhandle. Cotality (formerly CoreLogic), estimated that insured losses from wind and storm surge from hurricane Isaias will be in the $2 billion to $3 billion range.

Hurricane Simon track remained outside of Mexico cat bond parametric trigger zones: Loconte

As major hurricane Simon’s path shifted west it appears to have remained offshore of the important parametric trigger zones related to the $175 million IBRD CAR Mexico 2024 (Pacific) catastrophe bond, according to analysis by Isacco Loconte an insurance-linked securities (ILS) Investment Specialist at asset manager Azimut Switzerland SA. As we had been reporting over the weekend , hurricane Simon’s minimum central pressure, a critical factor in determining whether any payout would come due for the catastrophe bond, did deepen to a sufficiently low level to activate a payout should the storm’s track have crossed into any of the parametric trigger boxes. While those pressure levels were strong enough to meet the L1 payout threshold for the parametric zones, the hurricane trajectory worked in the bond’s favor.” Based on the parametric trigger design, hurricane Simon’s central pressure would have needed to be at 932mb or lower to activate a payout from the zones it came closest to.

3. 🌪️ Clima & Catástrofes

Mid-single-digit billions of dollars loss from Isaias unlikely to affect P&C re/insurer credit profiles, says Fitch

A wide rage of insurers and reinsurers are expected to be impacted by Hurricane Isaias, which made landfall as a Category 2 storm in the Florida Panhandle on October 9th, but while the event is expected to drive moderate economic and insured losses, Fitch says it is “not likely to affect credit for rated property/casualty (P/C) (re)insurers.” Hurricane Isaias peaked as a Category 3 major hurricane over the Gulf before increasing wind shear weakened it on approach, eventually making landfall as a Category 2 storm with maximum sustained winds of 105 mph, although has now been downgraded to a tropical storm. In affected areas like Florida, Alabama, Georgia, and The Carolinas, Fitch expects the storm to result in moderate economic and insured losses, estimating that industry losses could reach the mid-single-digit billions of dollars. “The storm’s reduced intensity at landfall is expected to result in more manageable insured losses,” says Fitch.

4. 💰 Inversión

First American backs Vesta’s $30 million Series B

Title insurer First American Financial Corporation participated in a $30 million Series B raised by Vesta , a mortgage software company developing AI agents to automate loan origination. The financing brings Vesta’s total funding to $85 million. First American’s participation gives the title insurer exposure to mortgage automation, including workflows that coordinate with settlement and insurance providers.
