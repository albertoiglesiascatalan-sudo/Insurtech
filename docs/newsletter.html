Thailand Shifts Disaster Risk to Insurers With $467 Million Plan

Thailand plans to provide government-funded disaster insurance to about 30 million households in high-risk areas, while seeking to shift some of the growing cost of floods and other catastrophes to private insurers. Thailand currently spends at least 30 billion baht a year compensating households for disaster losses, according to government estimates. Under the plan, insurers would take responsibility for payouts beyond the limits covered directly by the government, potentially reducing the taxpayers’ cost of major catastrophes.

SEADRIF makes over $1 million in payouts to Lao PDR and WFP under parametric policies

The Southeast Asia Disaster Risk Insurance Facility (SEADRIF Insurance Company) has made payouts of USD 1.14 million to the Government of Lao PDR and the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP), after heavy rainfall and widespread flooding triggered both Lao PDR’s two-year parametric sovereign policy and WFP’s 2026 disaster resilience policy. Prolonged southwest monsoon conditions and active low-pressure systems saw more than 260,000 people impacted by heavy rainfall and widespread flooding across Lao PDR, surpassing the 200,000-person mark reported by the Lao PDR National Disaster Management Office (NDMO), which triggered a first payout threshold under both SEADRIF policies. Following verification, SEADRIF paid out USD 1 million to the Government of Lao PDR, under its USD 8 million annual coverage, and USD 137,500 to WFP, under its USD 1.1 million policy.
